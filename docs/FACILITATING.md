# Facilitating an exercise

For whoever runs a session. Setting up the Mac is in
`docs/FACILITATING-ON-A-MAC.md`; what does not work yet is in
`docs/ROUGH-EDGES.md`.

**About the timings.** The durations below are the ones in the exercise
definitions and the Center's materials. No group has played the finished
table yet, so none of them is a measured result. After your first session,
please tell the author what the real times were and this page will be
corrected.

## Before anyone arrives (five minutes)

1. On the Mac, run `scripts/deploy.sh`. Note the address it prints.
2. Open `/health` on one tablet to prove it can reach the Mac.
3. Open the address, press "Start a table", and join with your name. Press
   "Become facilitator" if you want the admin seat; nothing requires it.
4. Copy the invite link and put it where people will see it (the call's chat).
5. For a class of several tables, press "Start a class" instead of "Start
   the Value Cards Trading", and choose the number of tables.

## The first two minutes

People arrive over a stretch of minutes and some will fight with the link.

- Keep the call open and talk. Say what the exercise is for, in a sentence,
  and that nobody wins it.
- Everyone who opens the link lands in the open sandbox. Let them move
  cards and dice around; this is how people learn to drag, turn a card
  over, and zoom. It costs nothing and it is what the sandbox is for.
- Ask each person who has joined to say their name aloud, so you can match
  names on the table to faces on the call.
- Do not begin the exercise until everyone who will play is seated and has
  moved something.

## Value Cards Trading

Exactly three people per table; any number of tables. About 30 minutes,
plus the debrief.

1. Press "Start the Value Cards Trading" (or "Start a class"), and sign people
   up. Deal. Everyone reads their opening hand.
2. Trading: people swap cards across the table and say what they are
   seeking and what they can release. Ask them to narrate the trades; the
   reasons are the exercise.
3. Compare final hands and talk about what was kept, refused or assumed.
4. Open the debrief from the facilitator's view.

## Leadership and Knowledge in Popular Rule

Nine to twenty people per city; several cities can run at once. About 45
minutes plus the debrief.

1. Use "Start a class", choose the number of cities, and sort the room into
   them (random first, then adjust by hand).
2. Each person takes a role and reads what only they know. Do not tell
   anyone what to share.
3. Run the timer from the class controls; push a prompt to every table at
   once when you want to change the question.
4. For a scenario that pairs two cities, each table proposes its choice and
   you confirm it.
5. Debrief: reconstruct the decision together. Whose knowledge travelled,
   whose did not, and why.

## Three failure modes to watch for

1. **One person is lost in the interface.** They stop talking, or sit with
   cards unturned. Ask by name, on the call, whether they can see their
   hand. A tablet that dropped Wi-Fi shows a reconnecting notice; waiting
   ten seconds usually fixes it, and reloading the page keeps the seat.
2. **The group performs the task and skips the reasoning.** Trades happen
   in silence, or a decision is made by whoever speaks first. Pause the
   table and ask someone to say why.
3. **Someone leaves or the address changes.** A person who leaves a begun
   table can be replaced: move a newcomer in from the facilitator's view.
   If the Mac's address changes, rerun `scripts/deploy.sh` and share the
   new address; tables return as they were.

## When the table goes quiet

Silence on a call is often thinking. Give it a minute. If it goes on, ask a
question from the exercise's prompts (the facilitator's notes on each
exercise page have some), or ask "what is hard to decide right now?". Do not
fill the silence with your own opinion.
